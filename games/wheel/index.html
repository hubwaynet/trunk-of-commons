CommonWeal

How it works

Six seats round a turning wheel. You sit at the bottom and start at the white arrow. The wheel turns 6° a second unless votes change it, so both arrows travel round the table.

The black side pays. Each second, whoever has the black arrow pays 9 tokens, and their two neighbours pay 1 each.

The white arrow collects. What's paid crosses the wheel in 2 seconds and goes to whoever has the white arrow when it arrives.

Share what you receive. Choose how much to pass on, and to how many of the people nearest you. Shares travel round the wheel to them, each player's in their own lane. The boost fund, in the hub, adds up to 10% to what you share.

Keeping costs. A fee goes to the boost fund on whatever you keep: 95% if you share nothing, half if you share half. Sharing more keeps the fee down.

Steer the wheel. A vote with the wheel's turn speeds it up, for ¼ token a second per degree. A vote against slows it, never below 2° a second, and costs four times as much: a token a degree, so holding an arrow back costs more than it brings. Turning it round takes more than 9 against, so nobody can do it alone. Your vote fades back to 0 one step a second, and each seat's vote shows as a curved arrow beside it.

Last six minutes. The table ends early if anyone can't pay. If it lasts and the poorest player still has a tenth of the stake, the boost fund is shared out by sharing: in proportion to how much of what reached each player they passed on, so a keeper gets none of it. Everyone also gets a bonus from the common fund.

Reputation above average about average below average Reputation here is relative, as in the 2017 design: each seat's disc compares the part of what reached them that they have passed on so far with the table's average. Within 5 points counts as about average. (Centipede & Ultimatum's rings judge each player on their own; these judge them against the table.)

Lower stakes make the six minutes harder to reach: at 500 your dials decide most.

TRUNK OF COMMONSAbout & credits

Educational social dilemma games about trust, cooperation, reputation and the commons. Designed by Jason Lee Lasky at hubway.net, Northern Rivers, NSW, from his design notes TrunK of Commons (2018) and the Wheel of Fortune Sharing Game (2017).

Draws on Robert Axelrod's iterated prisoner's dilemma tournaments and Elinor Ostrom's work on governing the commons.

Built with AI agents under human direction: code co-written with Claude (Anthropic), every change reviewed and published by Jason.
