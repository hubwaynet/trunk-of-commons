Centipede & Ultimatum

How it works

Take turns: give or grab. Each turn you give the round's amount (1 in round 1, one more each round) or grab the whole pool. If nobody grabs, the Commons adds 1 for each giver and the pool carries on growing. After each split, and at the start, the pool must go once round the table before anyone can grab.

The grabber divides. They set every player's share, rewarding or shortchanging whoever they like. Then everyone votes: 0 rejects (free), 1 accepts, 2 welcomes. Votes are paid from your share. An average of 1.5 or more welcomes the split, and the Commons adds 1 point for every player.

Average below 1: rejected. The grabber leaves the table, the pool goes back to each player in proportion to what they put in, and everything the grabber holds stays in the pool for the others. Otherwise the split is paid out.

Can't afford the amount? You pass: no giving, no grabbing, but you keep your seat and your vote. The rising amount is this game's real pressure: every round costs one more. There's no last round. When fewer than three players can afford the amount, the pool goes to a closing split: the next player in turn divides it, even if they can't afford the amount, and everyone votes. If the table welcomes it and three or more can then afford the next round, play carries on; otherwise the table is over. It also ends when fewer than three remain.

Free riders. A 0 vote on a fair split that passed anyway is a free 0. Fair means you got at least what you put in and close to an equal share, nobody else was short-changed, and the divider kept no more than a fair share. A 0 against an unfair split is a protest, not a free 0. Two free 0s make a free rider: anyone dividing can cut their share, and nobody defends them. The divide card shows who is on one free 0 and who is a free rider.

Rings generous (double) cooperative (thin) free loading (dashed) exploitive (heavy) From each player's splits and votes so far: exploitive kept more than a fair share in their latest split; free loading has two free 0s; generous gave part of their own share away, or votes 2 on most splits.

TRUNK OF COMMONSAbout & credits

Educational social dilemma games about trust, cooperation, reputation and the commons. Designed by Jason Lee Lasky at hubway.net, Northern Rivers, NSW, from his design notes TrunK of Commons (2018) and the Wheel of Fortune Sharing Game (2017).

Draws on Robert Axelrod's iterated prisoner's dilemma tournaments and Elinor Ostrom's work on governing the commons.

Built with AI agents under human direction: code co-written with Claude (Anthropic), every change reviewed and published by Jason.

Bots are named at random each table, so reading the code won't tell you who is who.
